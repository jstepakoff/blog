Homeowners in Porter Ranch, Northridge, Granada Hills, and Chatsworth want to know why the number is what it is, when it is due, and whether there is anything they can do about it.

## How your property tax is calculated

California property taxes are built on Proposition 13. The basic levy is 1% of your home's assessed value. On top of that, most bills include voter-approved charges, like bonds for local schools and community colleges, plus direct assessments for things such as lighting, flood control, or a Mello-Roos district.

The key word is assessed value. That is not the same as what your home would sell for today. When you buy a home, the assessed value is generally set at the purchase price. After that, it can only go up by the rate of inflation, capped at 2% per year, no matter how much the market rises.

That is why two identical homes on the same street can have very different tax bills. The neighbor who bought in 2005 is paying tax on a much lower assessed value than the neighbor who bought last year.

## The dates that matter

The LA County Treasurer and Tax Collector splits the annual secured tax bill into two installments:

- **First installment:** due November 1 and late after December 10.
- **Second installment:** due February 1 and late after April 10.

If a deadline lands on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day. Miss one, and the county adds a 10% penalty, so it is worth putting both dates on your calendar.

If your lender pays through an impound account, you usually don't need to do anything, but it is worth confirming the payment posted.

## The savings people miss

**The homeowners' exemption.** If the home is your primary residence, you can claim the homeowners' exemption. It takes $7,000 off your assessed value, which works out to roughly $70 a year. It is free money, and plenty of people never file after they buy.

**A decline-in-value review.** Under Proposition 8, if the market value of your home drops below its assessed value, you can ask the Assessor to temporarily lower your assessment. This mostly matters for people who bought near a market peak. The Assessor takes these requests during a window each year that closes at the end of November. **Transfers for homeowners 55 and older.** Proposition 19 lets eligible homeowners 55 and older move their assessed value to a replacement home anywhere in California, with some limits. ## What happens when you buy or remodel

**Buying.** When a home changes hands, the assessed value resets based on the purchase price. A few months after closing, most buyers get a supplemental tax bill that covers the difference between the old assessment and the new one for the rest of the tax year. It surprises a lot of people, so plan for it.

**Remodeling.** New construction gets reassessed too, but only the added value, not the whole house. Add a bedroom or a bathroom with permits, and the Assessor adds value for that new work. Your original assessed value on the rest of the home stays the same.

Skipping permits to avoid that is never worth it. Unpermitted work can cause real trouble when you sell.

## Why your bill changed

If this year's bill looks different from last year's, it is usually one of these:

- The normal annual increase of up to 2%
- A new or updated voter-approved bond or direct assessment
- A purchase or permitted remodel that triggered a reassessment
- A homeowners' exemption that was dropped or never filed

If none of those explain it, the Assessor's office can tell you exactly what changed.

## Frequently asked questions

### When are Los Angeles County property taxes due?

The first installment is due November 1 and becomes late after December 10. The second installment is due February 1 and becomes late after April 10.

### How much is property tax on a house in Porter Ranch?

The base rate is 1% of your assessed value, plus voter-approved bonds and any direct assessments. Some newer Porter Ranch communities also carry Mello-Roos, which can add a meaningful amount. When I work with buyers, I look up the actual bill for each home so there are no surprises.

### Does a remodel increase my property taxes in California?

Usually only for the value the new work adds. A permitted addition or new bathroom gets assessed, but the rest of your home keeps its existing assessed value.

### What is the homeowners' exemption in Los Angeles County?

It is a $7,000 reduction in assessed value for your primary residence, which saves you roughly $70 a year. You file once with the Assessor, and it stays in place as long as you live there.
