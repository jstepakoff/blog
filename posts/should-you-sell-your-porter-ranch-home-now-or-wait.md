I was looking at Google search trends recently, and one thing jumped out at me. Searches for "is it a good time to sell a house" are up 550% over the past five years, and the different versions of "sell now or wait" are climbing faster than almost any other real estate question. It lines up with what I am hearing in person. This is the question Porter Ranch homeowners keep asking me lately, at kitchen tables, at open houses, and over coffee. People are not asking because they have to move. They are asking because they honestly cannot tell whether this market rewards patience or punishes it.

So here is my honest take, based on the conversations I have been having and on what the numbers actually say about our neighborhood right now.

**Short answer:** I do not see a strong reason to wait for spring. Porter Ranch homes sold in September 2026 at a median price of $1,192,500 and closed at 99.3% of asking price, and the latest forecasts do not show mortgage rates dropping meaningfully in 2027. If your own life is not ready, wait. Just know the market itself is not paying sellers to hold out.

## The Porter Ranch numbers right now

- **Median sale price:** $1,192,500 in September 2026 (MLS data via Realty.com). Redfin puts the median sale price at about $1.21 million for the three months ending September 2026, and Zillow's typical home value for Porter Ranch is about $1.25 million as of September 30, 2026.
- **Sale to list ratio:** Homes sold at 99.3% of asking price in September 2026 (MLS). Buyers negotiate a little. Well-priced homes still close essentially at asking.
- **Days on market:** The median Porter Ranch listing took 31 days to sell in September 2026 (CRMLS data).
- **Inventory:** 127 homes were for sale in Porter Ranch at the end of September 2026, with a median list price of $1,439,800 (MLS). You have more competition than you would have had two years ago.
- **Price direction:** Porter Ranch prices are down about 5% year over year on a repeat-sales basis (county assessor data via Realty.com). We are cooling off from the peak. We are not crashing.
- **Mortgage rates:** The average 30-year fixed rate was 7.32% in Freddie Mac's October 22, 2026 survey, and 7.36% on October 26 according to Bankrate. Fannie Mae's latest forecast has rates staying in the 7.50% range through 2027.

## The case for selling now

**The buyers who are out there are serious.** Thirty Porter Ranch homes sold in September, and one in five sold for cash. Fall buyers are usually moving for jobs, schools, or life changes. They are not browsing.

**You skip the spring crowd.** Every year, sellers wait for spring because they think everyone else is wrong to wait. Then they all list at once. A good home listed now competes with far fewer similar homes.

**Prices have already slipped about 5% from last year.** If that pace continues, a year of waiting costs you more than it makes you. Rates are not forecast to save you either.

## The case for waiting

**Spring does bring the biggest buyer pool.** If your home shows best with spring landscaping and longer days, and you are in no hurry, a March to May listing will get more eyes on it. Just go in knowing you will have more competition too.

**Waiting is smart if the home is not ready.** I say this to every seller I meet: buyers in this market pay full price for the right home and negotiate hard on everything else. Unfinished projects and deferred maintenance come straight out of your pocket. A few months spent getting the home truly ready beats a rushed listing every time.

## The factor that matters most

Honestly, it is not in any of the numbers above. The most important factor in when to sell is whether it is the right time for you. A new job, a growing family, a home that no longer fits, wanting to be closer to your kids or your parents. Those are the reasons people actually move, and they do not wait for a perfect rate or a perfect season. The market should inform your timing at the margins, a few weeks here or a season there. It should never make the decision for you. If it is the right time for you, we can make this market work. If it is not, no stat I show you should talk you into moving.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I wait for mortgage rates to drop before selling?

I would not build a plan around it. Fannie Mae projects the 30-year fixed rate will average around 7.50% through 2027, which is slightly higher than where we are today. And think about the other side of it: if rates did drop sharply, every homeowner who has been waiting would list at the same time, and your competition would spike. If you are selling and buying in the same market, the rate hits both sides of your move equally anyway.

### Is it a good time to sell a house in Porter Ranch?

Yes, if the home is ready and priced from real comparable sales. September's numbers show buyers paying 99.3% of asking and a median of 31 days on market. That is a balanced market that works fine for a prepared seller. It is not 2021, and nobody should price like it is. It is not a buyer's market either. Pricing and condition decide who wins now.

### What is the best time to sell a house in Porter Ranch?

Spring, if you only look at buyer traffic. But the best time for your home depends on what you are competing against. A home with almost no similar listings in the fall can do better than the same home buried in spring inventory. I pay more attention to the competition on your street than to the calendar.

## The bottom line

Here is how I put it when someone asks me directly: Porter Ranch sellers are still getting strong prices, close to asking, in about a month on market. Rates are not forecast to rescue anyone who waits. If your life timing says move, the market supports it. If your home needs work, use the winter, get it ready, and list into spring with confidence. Either way, run the numbers on your own house before you decide. National headlines will not tell you what your home is worth.
